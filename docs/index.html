A punch clock that knows nothing about you.

One button, «Punch in/out», and a list of your times. Every punch is encrypted on your phone before it leaves it — the server stores only ciphertext and never sees a working time. No e-mail address, no real name.

One button. That is all.

There is only one kind of stamp: a point in time. What it means follows from the order.

Punch with an NFC sticker

Stick an NFC sticker on your locker, desk, notebook or bag, hold your phone to it, and the punch is made. On an iPhone your work badge often does the job too, if it has an NFC chip: hold the badge to the phone instead of the phone to a sticker. Nothing on the badge is changed. The sticker only opens a link that is the same for everyone and holds nothing about you; it only works on a device where you are logged in. The punch is made at once; if you prefer, the app asks «Punch now?» first. Step-by-step instructions for iPhone and Android are in the settings.

Badge reminder

Does your badge need validating at the reception every so often? Turn on the reminder: at the earliest every 25 calendar days (or as many as you choose), the first punch of the day brings up a note once. The count starts when you switch it on, so the first note comes after 25 days. If you do not punch that day, it comes with the next first punch.

One button: «Punch in/out»

Tap it when you start, tap it when you stop. The start screen shows where you are: in since when, or out, and today’s total. Above it are the punches of one day with its total — today, or swipe to earlier days. Handy for copying them into another system.

Punches pair up in order

Within a day, the 1st punch is in, the 2nd out, the 3rd in again, and so on: 08:02 – 12:01, 12:48 – 17:10. Add, change or delete one, and the day pairs up anew, in plain sight.

Day and week totals

The list shows every day with its pairs and total, and every week with its sum against your target. Each punch has its own row with a pencil to change it and a bin to delete it; the bin in a day’s header deletes the whole day, after asking once. Forgot one? Add it with «+»; the time is set on a wheel for hours and minutes.

Night shifts, if you need them

An optional setting moves the change of day to a time you choose, e.g. 04:00. A shift over midnight then stays one day.

Absences and a sheet to print

Vacation, sick, a public holiday or something else: mark a whole or half day in the list, or a from–until range, one entry per weekday. An absence counts towards your target time (a whole day is a fifth of your weekly hours), so the week sums and the balance stay right; it is encrypted and synced like a punch. For your employer or your files, open a week or month sheet: one row per day with the in–out times, absences and totals, then worked time, credit, target and balance. Print it or save it as a PDF, black on white, A4.

Nerd mode

Balance for the week, the month and the last 12 months, hours per week (13 weeks) and per month (13 months), average first and last punch, breaks, and a heatmap of your punches by weekday and hour, over 90 days or all 13 months.

Offline, and like an app

Add it to your home screen; the settings show how, on iPhone, iPad and Android. Without a connection you keep punching; the punches wait on the phone and go out when you are online again.

What the server knows

Your browser derives the keys from your passphrase and encrypts every punch (AES-256-GCM, padded to a fixed size) before it is sent. The server keeps what it needs to let you log in and to sync — and nothing it could read.

The server seesThe server never sees

  • The server seesan HMAC of your username (and the username itself, in transit, while you register or log in)

    The server never seesyour username at rest

  • The server seesHMACs of the login keys, and your master key, wrapped

    The server never seesyour passphrase, the unwrapped master key

  • The server seesfixed-size ciphertext per punch

    The server never seestimes, notes, settings

  • The server seeswhen encrypted writes arrive; it stores only days, never a time of day: the UTC day of a record’s last write, of your last use and of a session’s last use

    The server never seeswhat they contain

  • The server seesyour IP address, only in passing: as a keyed hash in rate-limit counters that expire within two days

Honest limits

  • You must trust the JavaScript the server delivers. Like every web app that encrypts in the browser, a malicious or compromised server could ship code that leaks your passphrase. What helps: no third-party code, a strict Content Security Policy, no build step (what is in the repository is what runs), public source.
  • Arrival times are visible. Punching live sends a record at that moment, so the operator could guess your times. Punching without a network blurs that: the record then goes when you are back online.
  • A lost passphrase cannot be recovered. There is no back door, no reset by e-mail, no support that can restore your data. The punches stay unreadable, and the account is deleted after 61 days without use.
  • Usernames are not secret. Registering tells you whether a name is taken. Pick one that does not identify you.
  • A long passphrase matters. Whoever obtained the database and the server secret could try passphrases offline, at 600 000 PBKDF2 rounds per guess.

No e-mail, no real name

An account is two things — and only you know the second one.

  1. A username

    3–32 characters, a–z 0–9 . _ -. No real name needed — better none. On a new device it is all you need besides the passphrase.

  2. A passphrase

    At least 6 characters, better 10 or more; several words work well. It never leaves your device: the browser turns it into one key to log in and another that unlocks your data. Changing it in the settings logs out your other devices.

  3. No reset

    Nobody can reset your passphrase — not even us. If it is lost, the punches can no longer be read, and the account is deleted automatically after 61 days without use; until then the username stays taken. So pick one you will remember.

Limits, stated plainly

Zeropunch keeps as little as it can, for as short as it can.

13months per punch

A punch is kept 13 months, then deleted for good — on your device and on the server. Export first what you want to keep: CSV or JSON. A wrong clock on your device deletes nothing.

12punches a day

12 punches per day in the app; once a day has 12, the button waits for the next day. The server accepts at most 100 record writes per account and day, so you can still add or fix several past days in one go.

61days without use

An account that is not used for 61 days is deleted, with all its punches and sessions. The app says so before it matters, and tells you when you come back after a long break.

Free and open source

Zeropunch is free software under the AGPL-3.0: no trackers, no cookies except the login session. Every line is on GitHub. You can also run your own copy: a small serverless backend with a SQL database, no build step. The README explains every step.

Self-hosting guide

Questions

Can anyone see my working times?

Not on the server. Every punch is encrypted in your browser before it leaves the device; the server stores fixed-size ciphertext. It does see when an encrypted write arrives. Punching without a network blurs that: the record then goes when you are back online.

What if I forget my passphrase?

Then your punches cannot be read any more — by anyone. Nobody can reset a passphrase: there is no back door, no reset by e-mail and no recovery key. The account is deleted automatically after 61 days without use; until then the username stays taken. While you are still logged in, you can change the passphrase in the settings (that needs the current one).

I punched wrong. Can I fix it?

Yes, right in the list: every punch has a pencil to change it and a bin to delete it once you confirm. A whole day wrong? The bin in the day’s header asks once and deletes all its punches. You set the time on a wheel for hours and minutes. Forgot a punch? Add it with «+», up to 13 months back. The day then pairs up anew.

Do I have to install an app?

No. Zeropunch runs in the browser. You can add it to your home screen (the settings show how on iPhone, iPad and Android), and it keeps punching without a connection; the punches are sent once you are online again.

How long are my punches kept?

13 months, then they are deleted for good. Export what you want to keep before that, as CSV or JSON. An account that is not used for 61 days is deleted with everything in it.

Can I punch with an NFC sticker?

Yes. Stick an NFC sticker on your locker, desk, notebook or bag and hold your phone to it. The punch is made at once; if you prefer, the app asks «Punch now?» first. The sticker only opens a link that is the same for everyone and holds nothing about you. On an iPhone your work badge often does the job too, if it has an NFC chip: hold the badge to the phone instead of the phone to a sticker. Nothing on the badge is changed. Step-by-step instructions for iPhone and Android are in the settings.

Can I record holidays and sick days and print my times?

Yes. Vacation, sick days, public holidays and other absences are entered as a whole or half day, at most 31 days ahead; they count like working time towards your target. For every week and month there is a sheet to print or save as PDF.

I work night shifts. Does that work?

Yes. Turn on night shifts in the settings and choose when your day changes, for example at 04:00. Punches before that time count to the previous day, so a shift over midnight stays one day.

Is it free? Can I run it myself?

Yes. Zeropunch is free and open source under the AGPL-3.0. For a copy of your own you need a small serverless backend with a SQL database. The README on GitHub explains the setup.
